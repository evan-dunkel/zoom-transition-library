When you're done, press Copy results at the bottom and paste the text back to me. Your answers are kept if you reload this page in the same browser. The dark bar at the bottom of each frame is a diagnostic I added (it's not part of the library): it shows whether anything was left behind after a transition, the slowest frame, and where keyboard focus is.

1. Card grid zooming into detail pages

A grid of walks. Click a picture: it flies into a detail card while the card grows around it. The neighbouring walks peek in at the sides (← → or a sideways trackpad swipe moves between them). Close with Esc, the ✕, a click on the dimmed area, or by scrolling up past the top of the card.

What to look for

  1. Should work The picture leaves exactly from its thumbnail and lands exactly at the top of the card, with no jump at either end. (Turn on slow motion at the top to see it clearly.)
  2. Should work Closing sends the picture back into its own thumbnail, and the page looks exactly as before.
  3. Fixed: confirm At the moment you click, the other thumbnails no longer blink out: they fade away as the card opens, and fade back as it closes.
  4. Fixed: confirm In slow motion, the ✕ no longer floats outside the growing card; it appears only once the card has grown to where it sits.
  5. Fixed: confirm Only the visible card shows a ✕; the neighbours peeking in at the sides don't.
  6. Fixed: confirm Page turns (← →) settle quickly, without a long slow creep at the end. The new default is 0.25 s with no bounce; the “Page turn” switch at the top lets you compare it with 0.35 s and the old 0.5 s.
  7. Your judgement The motion feels good to you overall: speed, slight bounce, the way the card grows out of the picture.

2. A scrolled page with a sticky header

A long page whose header stays stuck to the top. The cards are further down: scroll to “Featured walks” before opening one.

What to look for

  1. Should work After scrolling, the zoom still starts exactly from the thumbnail you clicked.
  2. Should work After closing, you are at the same scroll position as before.
  3. Fixed: confirm Scroll so a thumbnail is half hidden under the header, then click its title. The flying picture now stays below the header at take-off (and on landing), instead of popping out on top of it.
  4. Still a limitation In that same case, the faint neighbouring cards can still pass over the header for a moment as they fade in.

3. Narrow phone-width layout (390 px)

A phone-width list with small square thumbnails that open into full-height cards: a big change of shape. For touch, use “Open in new tab” and your browser's device mode (Chrome: Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+M), or open the file on a phone.

What to look for

  1. Fixed: confirm First open after loading: the neighbouring cards now fade in smoothly from the start, instead of appearing already partway in. (Reload this frame to try a first open again.)
  2. Should work The picture grows from the small square into the wide picture at the top of the card, its crop changing smoothly with no jump.
  3. Should work Closing shrinks it back into its own square.
  4. Your judgement (Touch, optional) Dragging down from the top of the card closes it; dragging sideways moves to the next walk.

4. Reduced motion (simulated in this frame)

This frame pretends your device has “Reduce motion” turned on. To test the real setting, turn it on (instructions at the bottom of this page) and use the other frames: they follow your device setting.

What to look for

  1. Should work Opening is instant: the card is simply there. Nothing flies, grows or fades.
  2. Should work Closing is instant too, and ← → switch walks without sliding.
  3. Your judgement (Optional) With the real device setting turned on, frames 1–3 also open and close instantly, without reloading this page.

5. Rapid repeated clicks, interruptions and shared links

The buttons in the frame run quick sequences by themselves: double-click, ten fast clicks, Esc or Back half way, clicking a card as it flies home, random input. Try the same by hand too, including in very slow motion. Browser history is on here, so Back closes the card, and each open card has its own #address.

What to look for

  1. Should work After each sequence settles, the dark status bar at the bottom says “clean ✓” (press Esc first if a card is left open).
  2. Fixed: confirm In very slow motion, click a card, Esc, click it again as it flies home, Esc… The cards now curve back smoothly instead of stopping dead and jumping left and right.
  3. Fixed: confirm While typing in a note field on this page, the frames no longer pull focus away from you when their transitions finish.
  4. Fixed: confirm Press “Open in new tab”, open a card there, then reload that tab: the card is open again. Close it: the #… disappears from the address.
  5. Your judgement Nothing ever gets stuck, flickers, or ends up in the wrong place.

6. Detail photos that are still downloading

On a real site the thumbnail and the big detail photo are different files, and the big one only starts downloading when you click. Here the big photos take 1.5 seconds to arrive the first time each card opens (they're remembered after that).

What to look for

  1. Fixed: confirm First open of each card: the thumbnail's own picture now flies into the card (instead of an empty grey box), and the big photo takes over when it arrives.
  2. Should work Second open of the same card: the picture flies as before.
  3. Still a limitation The neighbouring cards' photos are still downloading too, so their picture areas stay plain grey for a moment.

7. Content inside a card that scrolls sideways

The detail cards contain a photo strip and a wide table, each meant to scroll sideways on its own. Open a card, scroll down a little to them, and scroll them sideways (two-finger sideways swipe on a trackpad, or Shift + mouse wheel).

What to look for

  1. Fixed: confirm The strip and the table now scroll sideways; the card stays put.
  2. Should work A sideways swipe elsewhere on the card (over the text) still moves to the next walk.
  3. Your judgement When the strip reaches its end mid-swipe, the rest of that swipe doesn't turn the page; a new swipe does. Does that feel right?

8. A large gallery (48 walks)

Opening now builds only the card you opened and its two neighbours; the rest are built after the zoom lands, while nothing moves. Watch the “slowest frame” number in the status bar (17 ms is perfectly smooth; over about 50 ms is a visible hitch).

What to look for

  1. Fixed: confirm The picture starts moving as promptly here as in scenario 1 (9 walks), with no hesitation.
  2. Your judgement Note the “slowest frame” number after opening a card here, and after closing one.

How to turn on reduced motion on your device

Turn it on, come back to this page, and open a card in scenarios 1–3. They should open and close instantly. No reload is needed.

Mac

  • System Settings → Accessibility → Display → turn on Reduce motion.

iPhone / iPad

  • Settings → Accessibility → Motion → turn on Reduce Motion.

Windows 11

  • Settings → Accessibility → Visual effects → turn off Animation effects.

Windows 10

  • Settings → Ease of Access → Display → turn off Show animations in Windows.

Android

  • Settings → Accessibility → turn on Remove animations (on some phones: Accessibility → Visibility enhancements, or Color and motion).

Without changing your settings (Chrome or Edge)

  • Open DevTools (F12 or Cmd+Option+I), press Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+P, type reduced motion, and choose “Emulate CSS prefers-reduced-motion: reduce”. It applies to this tab only while DevTools is open.

The status bar inside each frame shows “reduced motion: on (your device)” when the setting is detected.
